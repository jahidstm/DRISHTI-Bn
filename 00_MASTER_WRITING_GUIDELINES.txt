RULE 1 — ORIGINAL WORDING (DIU similarity limit)
===========================================================================

DIU checks every thesis with Turnitin. Limits: total similarity at most 40%
and less than 3% from any single source. Aim for well below that.

  - Never copy more than 3 consecutive words from a paper, except technical
    names (e.g. "Vision Transformer", "Low-Rank Adaptation", dataset names).
  - Paraphrase by re-thinking the idea: change the sentence order, the
    grammatical subject and the way the argument is built, not just single
    words. Combine information from two or more sources in one paragraph
    instead of summarising one paper per paragraph.
  - Do not reuse the abstract sentences of a paper. Describe what the paper
    did and found, in your own structure, and relate it to this study.
  - Never write long definitions of standard concepts (DIU guideline:
    "Don't write definitions and don't prolong the discussion unnecessarily").
  - Quote only if wording itself matters; quotes need quotation marks and
    a [REF]. Avoid quotes in general.

===========================================================================
RULE 2 — CITATIONS: [REF] PLACEHOLDERS (APA will be added later in Zotero)
===========================================================================

  - Write [REF] directly after the claim it supports, not at the end of a
    long sentence that contains several claims.
        CORRECT:   ViT splits an image into 16 × 16 patches [REF], while
                   CNN models rely on local convolutions [REF].
        INCORRECT: ViT splits an image into patches and is better than CNNs. [REF]
  - Help the author find the source: when the claim comes from one of the
    uploaded papers, write the author and year inside the placeholder,
    e.g. [REF: Dosovitskiy et al., 2021] or [REF: CrisisMMD, Alam et al., 2018].
    If unsure which paper supports the claim, write only [REF].
  - Every factual claim about OTHER work needs a [REF]. Results of THIS
    study (from 02_RESULTS_FACT_SHEET.md) need no [REF].
  - Do not invent authors, years or paper titles. Cite only papers that
    exist in this notebook.
  - Prefer papers from 2020 onward; older papers only for foundational
    methods (Transformer, Grad-CAM, calibration, BLEU, etc.).
  - Each prompt states how many [REF] placeholders the section must contain.
    Stay inside that range.

RULE 3 — STYLE AND VOCABULARY
===========================================================================

  - Vary sentence length and structure. Mix short, direct sentences with
    longer technical ones. Avoid starting consecutive sentences the same way.
  - Avoid empty filler words and phrases. Do not use:
      delve, crucial, pivotal, robust (use "stable" or give the number),
      seamless, notably, furthermore, moreover (at most once per section),
      in conclusion, it is worth noting, it is important to note,
      plays a vital/crucial role, leverage (verb), underscore (verb),
      landscape / paradigm (as metaphors), cutting-edge, groundbreaking,
      revolutionary, novel (unless stated by the fact sheet), state-of-the-art
      (unless comparing with a cited benchmark number).
  - Be concrete: give the number instead of an adjective
    ("Macro F1 of 75.69 ± 1.33%" instead of "strong performance").
  - American English spelling throughout (analyze, color, modeling).
  - Write "Bengali" for the language; keep "BanglaBERT", "Bangla" only in names.
  - First mention of an abbreviation: full form followed by the abbreviation
    in parentheses, e.g. "Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)". Afterwards use only
    the abbreviation.

===========================================================================
RULE 4 — ACADEMIC TONE
===========================================================================

  - Third person: "this study", "the proposed framework", "the results show".
    Do not use "we", "our", "I".
  - Active voice by default; passive voice is acceptable for procedures
    ("The images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels.").
  - Match the strength of each claim to the evidence:
      strong evidence      → shows, demonstrates, confirms
      moderate evidence    → suggests, indicates, is consistent with
      weak / single seed   → may, might reflect, requires further study
  - Report uncertainty: give mean ± standard deviation, confidence intervals
    and the number of samples (n) where the fact sheet provides them.
  - Equations: numbered, variables in italics, dimensions stated,
    e.g. Q ∈ ℝ^(n × d_k). Write equations as [EQUATION X.Y: ...] placeholders
    in plain text so they can be typed in Word's equation editor.

===========================================================================

  - Every number about this study must come from 02_RESULTS_FACT_SHEET.md.
    If an older file, figure or paper shows a different number for this
    study, the fact sheet wins.
  - Never write the claims listed under "DO NOT CLAIM" in the fact sheet

  - Negative findings are part of the contribution. Report them plainly
    (e.g. generated captions imitate tweets; the classifier is image-driven).
  - If the sources do not contain the requested information, write
    [MISSING: short description] in the text instead of guessing.

RULE 7 — OUTPUT FORMAT
===========================================================================

  - Start with the section heading exactly as given in the prompt

RULE 8 — CONTENT ALIGNMENT (what this thesis is really about)
===========================================================================

  PROBLEM: Disaster images shared on social media could help responders,
  but (a) Bengali-language tools for this are almost absent, (b) many
  published results on CrisisMMD-type data are not checked for leakage,
  explanation quality or calibration, and (c) training data from Bangladesh
  are scarce.

  WHAT THIS STUDY DOES:
    1. Builds a Bengali-text version of the CrisisMMD humanitarian image
       subset (3,755 images, machine-translated tweets) with a leakage-free,
       tweet-grouped split.
    2. Adapts ViT-Base/16 and BanglaBERT with LoRA (1.38 M trainable
       parameters, ≈ 0.70%) for three-class damage classification and
       matches a ViT-Large baseline with 38× fewer trainable parameters.
    3. Tests whether the Bengali text helps (it does not; the classifier is
       image-driven) and whether a jointly trained decoder can describe
       images in Bengali (it imitates tweets instead).
    4. Checks explanations quantitatively (deletion test: Grad-CAM is
       faithful) and checks calibration (ECE 2.57%, already well calibrated).
    5. Runs a preliminary external test on 325 Bangladeshi flood and cyclone
       images labelled by three annotators.

  Frame every section around careful, honest evaluation of a
  parameter-efficient model — not around claims of superiority.
